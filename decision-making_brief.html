01What This Course Is

This course examines how people and organisations actually make decisions — under cognitive bias, ethical pressure, cultural expectation, and now, AI assistance. AI is a constant presence in the course, but it is never the point: the human decision-maker stays at the centre throughout. Every case, framework, and debate draws on Taiwan-based business and regulatory context.

02What You'll Be Able to Do

  • Tell the difference between a good decision process and a good decision outcome — and why that distinction matters
  • Spot and correct for the cognitive biases that quietly wreck individual and group decisions
  • Apply utilitarian, deontological, and virtue-based ethical frameworks to real business dilemmas — including ESG/CSR
  • Evaluate AI-assisted decisions using real frameworks, not gut feeling
  • Explain how and why unethical decisions actually happen inside organisations
  • Navigate cross-cultural decision-making in Taiwanese and international business contexts
  • Build and defend your own personal decision-making framework

About the Debates

Both the Mid-Term and Final-Term exams are live, in-class team debates — not take-home essays. You'll be placed on a team, given a preparation window, and expected to defend your position under questioning.

Fluency in English or Chinese is weighted low — content and reasoning matter far more than delivery polish. AI tools are permitted during the preparation window.

Full format, team structure, and scoring rubric are covered in class before each debate — not published here.
